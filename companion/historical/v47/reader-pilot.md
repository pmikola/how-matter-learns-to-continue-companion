# Six tasks for a real-reader pilot

**Status: materials prepared; no human-reader sessions or outcomes recorded.** This is a small editorial pilot, not a population estimate. The goal is to observe operations and points of confusion, including disagreement with the wider proposal.

Recruit six willing readers: two who mainly follow prose and graphs, two comfortable with elementary algebra, and two with scientific or technical experience. Record prior familiarity with the book and models rather than assuming those categories are homogeneous. Use anonymous session IDs, obtain permission to record responses, and let readers skip or stop.

## Session route

Allow about 45 minutes. Give the assigned blind card for Task 1 first, with no version explanation, key, or coaching. Record answer and time before discussion. For Tasks 2–6, allow the book and record the page the reader uses. After the first answer, one neutral prompt, “What in the supplied pages supports that?”, is allowed and logged. Do not score an answer after an explanatory hint as unassisted.

## Task 1. Operate a retained rule

Use the assigned [Card A](reader-pilot-card-a.md) or [Card B](reader-pilot-card-b.md). Two records contain three and two correct labels out of five. Both next reports are RIGHT; both start at three. Name each retained policy and chosen side. Only after those choices, reveal RIGHT as the resource side and ask for next amounts. Ask what this one action does and does not establish about 99.01%.

## Task 2. Change the definition of success

At twelve updates, report-ignoring continuation is about 61.23% and always-follow is 50.02%; at nineteen, they are about 49.66% and 49.93%. How can the ranking reverse while both probabilities fall? Name the full-history event and the role of an environment that persists within an episode. Ask which condition is reset before the orientation-reversal comparison.

## Task 3. Infer a set from a reduced record

Change the detector exercise: at time zero the amount is 100; there is no supply and the constant positive rate k is unknown. The exact detector reports one at time four and zero at time eight, using threshold 50. What is still inferable? Do not supply an amount ratio or a previous input rate. A qualitative answer may be recorded before algebra.

## Task 4. Separate decision and confidence

Both K = 3 and K = 5 store FOLLOW. Has the bit retained everything the two records said about orientation? Could it recover their original confidence? Could a posterior conditional on the bit nevertheless exist? Ask which question changed; use the exercise only after the first response.

## Task 5. Choose an appropriate repair

Two records have identical descriptors but targets 0.2 and 0.8. A predictor gives −0.1 to both. Which of clipping predictions, refitting within the same descriptors, or adding a feature can change the output range, the fit, or the distinction between records? What would still need untouched validation after finding a useful feature?

## Task 6. Assess the larger proposal

What did the book demonstrate? What sensing, interpretation, retention, action and physical support did it supply or leave unconstructed? Give one serious alternative to a single common account and a test that could discriminate it. Agreement with the author is not a success criterion.

## Retrieval, curiosity and retention

During Task 3, ask the reader to locate a useful earlier example. Alternate whether the first search uses the contents alone or the new return guide, and record time, destination and confusion. The second search is practice-contaminated and exploratory. At actual stopping points after Chapters 20 and 24, or in a later full-book pilot, ask what remains unanswered and whether the reader wants to continue; do not infer whole-book engagement from this short excerpt session. Ask Tasks 1 and 4 again after 48–72 hours without the book, recording actual delay and any intervening reading.

Use the [blank response form](reader-response-form.md). Give only assigned cards and prompts to readers. Keep the [facilitator mapping and rubric](reader-pilot-facilitator.md) withheld until answers are recorded. The key and calculated examples are not responses from participants.
